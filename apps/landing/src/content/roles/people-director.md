title: People Director
status: open
team: People
commitment: Volunteer
location: Remote, or NYC-based preferred — with travel for discernment, training, and chapter launches
hoursPerWeek: 10
reportsTo: Executive Director
worksWith:
  - Functional Directors (Music, Marketing, Development, Events, Finance)
  - Chapter Directors
manages:
  - Chapter Directors
  - The People team as it grows (coordinator, recruiters, onboarding lead, chapter coaches)
trialTrack: director
seatId: expansion_director
order: 1
postedAt: 2026-08-23

summary: >-
  The central seat responsible for building the people and leadership capacity
  through which Public Worship grows — recruiting, development, and the
  chapters that come out of both. Designed as the Executive Director's number two.

whyThisSeatExists: >-
  Public Worship is volunteer-led, so the thing that limits growth is not money
  or ideas. It is leadership and people capacity. Right now every significant
  people conversation runs through the Executive Director — every yes, every
  no, every "should we launch there yet." That does not scale, and it is not
  good for the team. People growth builds leadership capacity, leadership
  capacity builds organizational capacity, and healthy expansion follows from
  that. Chapters in new cities are the outcome, not the point.

outcomes:
  - outcome: One funnel that everyone who wants to join passes through
    doneWhen: >-
      Someone can apply, be interviewed against a shared standard, run a real
      trial, and get a yes, a no, a why, or a not-yet — without the ED touching
      it.
  - outcome: The tribal knowledge of what makes this team good, written down
    doneWhen: >-
      Interviews with the current team have been turned into an interview
      standard, an onboarding path, and a development rhythm a new director
      could actually run.
  - outcome: NYC formed as the first chapter before year-end 2026
    doneWhen: >-
      Every seat on that chapter's leadership team is filled by someone trained
      and ready, and the chapter can run a gathering when any one person is
      unavailable.
  - outcome: A leadership bench, not a list of names
    doneWhen: >-
      You can name who is ready for more responsibility, who is close, and
      what each of them is missing — and the directors above them are working
      on it.
  - outcome: A specific answer to "how are our people?"
    doneWhen: >-
      The answer comes with names, patterns, and what you are doing about the
      ones who are struggling — not a general impression.

authority:
  - Say no, or not yet, to a candidate — including one a director wants
  - Set the interview standard and the onboarding path the whole org uses
  - Call whether a chapter's people are ready to launch, or not yet
  - Choose and develop Chapter Directors, with the ED on the final call
  - Own team culture events — what they are, when, and what they cost within budget
  - Decide when a people problem is a systems problem, and change the system

responsibilities:
  - area: Recruiting and talent
    items:
      - Run the first funnel for anyone who wants to join — sourcing, applications, interviews, discernment, placement
      - Build the interview and discernment system out of what already works on this team, not out of a template
      - Recruit for central teams and for chapters, in the order the process says (in-house, then the interest pool, then a public call, then personal networks)
      - Spot high-potential future leaders early and say so out loud
      - Sit in personally on discernment for senior leadership and Chapter Director candidates
  - area: People and leadership development
    items:
      - Onboarding, role clarity, and expectations for every new person
      - Training systems, leadership development, and director development
      - Performance and development rhythms — the 1:1s and reviews that actually happen
      - Volunteer health and retention; noticing who is quietly carrying too much
      - Team culture events, as part of people health rather than as a favor to Events
      - Helping directors develop the people underneath them, instead of doing it for them
      - Fixing the system when people repeatedly struggle or fall through the cracks
  - area: Chapter development and expansion
    items:
      - Chapter Director recruiting, discernment, formation, and a real coaching cadence
      - Forming each chapter's leadership team (music, events/production, marketing, treasurer) — central does not staff every local seat
      - Chapter onboarding, training, launch readiness, and the City Launch Playbook
      - Chapter health and leadership succession inside chapters
      - The go / not-yet call on whether the people exist before a city launches

rhythms:
  - Weekly 1:1 with the Executive Director
  - A standing coaching cadence with each Chapter Director and key leader
  - A weekly pass through the hiring pipeline — nobody waits on us longer than a week for a first reply
  - "A monthly read on people health: who is thriving, who is struggling, who is ready for more"
  - "Quarterly: what the leadership bench looks like, and where the gaps are"

firstNinetyDays:
  - Interview the current team — every director, and a cross-section of everyone else — and write down what actually makes this team good
  - Turn that into a first interview standard and a first onboarding path, and run the next candidate through it
  - Take the whole candidate funnel off the Executive Director's desk
  - Name the seats NYC has to fill before year-end, and start filling them
  - Bring back one thing that is broken about how we develop people, with three options and a recommendation

required:
  - Spiritually mature and rooted in a local church you attend and give to
  - Genuinely good with people — able to see someone clearly and still be kind about it
  - Comfortable in ambiguity; able to build structure where none exists
  - Willing to make hard calls about fit, and to say why out loud
  - More interested in developing leaders than in managing tasks
  - Around 10 hours a week, honestly, plus recurring meetings

preferred:
  - Experience hiring, onboarding, or developing people — paid or volunteer
  - Experience starting something in a new city, campus, or context
  - NYC-based
  - "Comfort writing things down: process, standards, playbooks"

notThisRole:
  - Not HR administration. You build the function first; the admin gets delegated later so you can focus on judgment, discernment, and coaching
  - Not the manager of every volunteer. Functional Directors own their teams' work and performance
  - Not assessing functional excellence in Music, Marketing, Events, Finance, or Development
  - Not central fundraising, donor relationships, or day-to-day finance
  - Not launching cities because there is interest — only because the people are ready
  - Not a New York event lead wearing a second hat

successLooks:
  - Public Worship can grow without the Executive Director personally carrying every people conversation
  - Someone can answer "how are our people?" with specificity
  - A chapter launches because its people are ready, and everyone can see why that was true

growthPath: >-
  This seat can grow into a department — a People Coordinator, recruiters,
  an onboarding and development lead, chapter coaches. At the start you build
  it yourself. The role is designed as the ED's number two and could develop
  someone toward eventually succeeding the ED, though succession is never
  automatic.
---

There is no finished people system here, and no playbook waiting for you. The
first real work is interviewing the team we already have to figure out what
makes it good, and then turning that into something repeatable. You would be
building the function, not inheriting it. Someone who waits to be handed a
process will struggle in this seat; someone who has been quietly wishing
somebody would just build the thing will probably love it.

One more thing worth saying plainly: this is sacrificial extra work on top of a
local church commitment. Public Worship is not a church and is not trying to
be yours. Everyone here serves and gives at their own church first.
