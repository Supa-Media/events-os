# Employee Handbook

## 1 · About this handbook

**What it is.** A description of how Public Worship works with the people who
work for it — what the organization owes them, and what it expects in return.
It gathers in one place the practices already implied by the Bylaws and the
Operating Manual.

**What it is not.** It is **not an employment contract** and creates no
contractual rights, express or implied. It is **not a promise of employment for
any period**. The organization may change, add, or withdraw any provision at any
time, with or without notice, except the at-will provision in § 3.1, which may
be modified only in a writing signed by the Executive Director.

**Who it covers.** Everyone who works for the organization, in the way each
section says. Where a section applies only to employees, it says so. Volunteers
are covered by §§ 4, 9, 11 and the conduct standards of § 7; contractors by
§§ 5, 9, 11.

**If something conflicts.** The Bylaws control over this handbook. Applicable
law controls over both. A provision that is unlawful in a particular state is
inapplicable there and does not affect the rest.

**Questions.** Ask your supervisor, or the Executive Director.

## 2 · Who we are and what we expect

Public Worship exists to create holy experiences through music that ignite faith
in Jesus. Everyone who works here — paid or not — is part of that.

Four expectations underlie everything below.

**Own your yes.** Commitments are made deliberately and kept. If you cannot do
something, say so early — that is respected. Silence is not.

**Be honest about capacity.** People here serve alongside jobs, school, and
families. Overcommitting and disappearing costs the team more than declining
would have.

**Steward what isn't yours.** Every dollar was given by someone who believed in
this. Spend it like that is true.

**Assume the room is public.** Write emails, messages, and purpose lines as if
they will be read by a stranger — because the ledger is published, and they may
be.

## 3 · Employment basics *(employees)*

**3.1 At-will employment.** Employment with the organization is **at will**.
Either the employee or the organization may end the employment relationship at
any time, with or without cause and with or without notice. Nothing in this
handbook, in any offer letter, or in any statement by any representative creates
a contract of employment or alters at-will status. Only a written agreement
signed by the Executive Director, and authorized by the Board where the Bylaws
require it, can do that.

**3.2 Equal employment opportunity.** The organization provides equal
employment opportunity and prohibits discrimination and harassment on the basis
of race, color, national origin, ancestry, age, sex, pregnancy, disability,
genetic information, marital status, military or veteran status, or any other
characteristic protected by applicable federal, Maryland, or local law. This
applies to recruiting, hiring, compensation, assignment, training, promotion,
discipline, and separation.

**3.3 Religious character and religious preference.** The organization is a
religious organization. As permitted by Section 702 of Title VII of the Civil
Rights Act of 1964 and comparable Maryland law, it may prefer, in employment,
individuals who share its religious beliefs, and may require employees to
subscribe to its statement of faith and to conduct consistent with its religious
tenets, where those requirements are lawfully applied. Certain positions may be
ministerial, in which case the constitutional ministerial exception applies;
those positions are identified in their job descriptions.

> **Counsel flag.** The boundary between a lawful religious preference and
> unlawful discrimination on another protected basis is contested and moves with
> the case law. Any faith-based hiring or conduct requirement, and any
> designation of a role as ministerial, must be reviewed by counsel before it is
> applied.

**3.4 Reasonable accommodation.** The organization provides reasonable
accommodation for qualified individuals with disabilities, for pregnancy and
related conditions, and for sincerely held religious beliefs and practices,
absent undue hardship. Requests go to the Executive Director and are handled
confidentially through an interactive discussion.

**3.5 Immigration compliance.** Every employee must complete Form I-9 and
provide documentation establishing identity and authorization to work in the
United States, within the time the law allows.

**3.6 Classification.** Each position is classified as **exempt** or
**non-exempt** under the Fair Labor Standards Act and Maryland law, and as
full-time, part-time, or temporary. Classification is stated in the offer letter
and determines overtime eligibility and benefits. Classification is a legal
determination based on duties and pay, not a matter of title or preference.

**3.7 Introductory period.** New employees typically work an introductory period
of ninety (90) days, during which performance is reviewed closely. Completing it
does not change at-will status or guarantee continued employment.

**3.8 Personnel records.** The organization maintains a personnel file for each
employee. Employees should promptly report changes to name, address, phone,
emergency contact, or tax withholding. Employees may request to review their own
file by arrangement with the Executive Director.

## 4 · Volunteers

Most of the work of Public Worship is done by volunteers, including nearly all
chapter leadership. This section is the promise made to them.

**4.1 Volunteer status.** Volunteers serve freely, for religious, charitable,
and civic reasons, without expectation of compensation, and are **not employees**
for any purpose. Service may be ended by either side at any time.

**4.2 No compensation.** Volunteers receive no wages, salary, or stipend.
Reimbursement of actual, documented expenses (Operating Manual § 4.3) and
non-cash recognition of nominal value are permitted and do not create employment.

**4.3 Employees may not volunteer for their own work.** An employee may not
perform, as an unpaid volunteer, the same type of work they are paid to do. This
is a Fair Labor Standards Act requirement and is not waivable, however willing
the person is.

> **Counsel flag.** Stipends, honoraria, "gas money" that exceeds documented
> expenses, and free-or-discounted benefits given to volunteers can convert
> volunteer status into employment. Review any recurring payment to a volunteer
> with counsel before it starts.

**4.4 What volunteers are owed.** A clear seat with stated duties and powers; a
supervisor who answers; training through the Academy; the equipment and budget
the role needs; timely reimbursement; the same protection from harassment,
retaliation, and unsafe conditions as any employee; and honesty about what the
commitment actually requires.

**4.5 What volunteers owe.** The conduct standards in § 7, the confidentiality
and data rules in § 8, the safety and child-protection rules in § 9, and the
commitment they actually agreed to.

**4.6 Screening.** Volunteers in roles involving money, donor records, or
contact with minors or vulnerable adults are screened as § 9.3 requires before
they begin.

**4.7 Insurance.** Volunteers are generally not covered by workers'
compensation. The organization maintains liability coverage as the Board
determines; volunteers are not covered for their own injuries except as any
volunteer-accident policy in force provides.

## 5 · Contractors

**5.1 Independent contractors are not employees.** A contractor controls how and
when the work is done, supplies their own tools, may work for others, and is
engaged for a defined scope under a written agreement. Contractors receive no
employee benefits, no leave, and no workers' compensation coverage, and are
responsible for their own taxes.

**5.2 Before the first payment.** A signed agreement stating scope, deliverables,
timing, and fee; and a completed **Form W-9**. Both come before the work is paid
for — not at year end.

**5.3 Payment.** Through the contractor-payment process in Operating Manual
§ 4.4. The agreement is the substantiation. Payments of $600 or more in a
calendar year are reported on **Form 1099-NEC by January 31**.

**5.4 Classification is not a choice.** If the organization controls the manner,
hours, and means of someone's work, they are an employee regardless of what the
paperwork says. Misclassification creates back-tax, penalty, and wage-law
exposure. When it is genuinely unclear, ask counsel or the accountant before
engaging.

## 6 · Pay, hours, and expenses *(employees)*

**6.1 Pay periods.** Employees are paid at least twice per month, in accordance
with the Maryland Wage Payment and Collection Law, on the schedule stated in the
offer letter, by direct deposit where possible.

**6.2 Timekeeping.** Non-exempt employees must record all time actually worked,
each day, accurately. Falsifying time records is grounds for termination.
Working "off the clock" is prohibited — the organization will not accept unpaid
work from a non-exempt employee, and a supervisor may not request it.

**6.3 Overtime.** Non-exempt employees receive overtime at one and one-half
times the regular rate for hours worked over forty (40) in a workweek. Overtime
must be authorized in advance by the supervisor; unauthorized overtime is still
paid, and is addressed as a performance matter.

**6.4 Meal and rest breaks.** Breaks are provided as required by law and as the
work allows. A non-exempt employee who works through a meal break must record
that time.

**6.5 Deductions.** Only lawful deductions are made — taxes, withholding
ordered by law, and deductions the employee has authorized in writing.

**6.6 Travel and irregular hours.** Public Worship's work includes evenings and
weekends. Non-exempt employees are paid for compensable travel and event time
under the FLSA; talk to your supervisor before an event about what will be
compensable.

**6.7 Expenses.** Business expenses are incurred and reimbursed through the
processes in Operating Manual §§ 4.2–4.3 — organization card where possible,
receipt always, reimbursement where a personal payment was unavoidable. **Never
pay for organization expenses personally as a matter of course.**

**6.8 Final pay.** On separation, all wages due are paid on or before the next
regularly scheduled payday, as Maryland law requires.

## 7 · Conduct

**7.1 Standard.** Act in the organization's interest, treat people with dignity,
tell the truth, and do not use your position for private advantage.

**7.2 Anti-harassment.** The organization prohibits harassment of any kind,
including sexual harassment, by or against anyone connected to its work — staff,
volunteers, contractors, attendees, vendors, and partners. Prohibited conduct
includes unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, slurs,
intimidation, ridicule, offensive jokes, unwelcome touching, and displaying or
circulating offensive material. It applies at events, in messages, on
organization platforms, and off-site where connected to the work.

> **Counsel flag.** Maryland's harassment provisions reach employers with very
> few employees, and its general employment-discrimination provisions generally
> reach employers with 15 or more. Confirm current coverage thresholds with
> counsel. The organization's own prohibition applies regardless of headcount.

**7.3 Reporting.** Report harassment, discrimination, or retaliation to your
supervisor, the Executive Director, or — where the concern involves the
Executive Director, or you are uncomfortable reporting internally — directly to
the **Chair of the Board** or the **Treasurer**. Reports may be made verbally or
in writing. Every report is taken seriously, investigated promptly and as
confidentially as the investigation allows, and acted on. Bystanders and
supervisors who learn of possible harassment must report it.

**7.4 Non-retaliation.** Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in
good faith, or who participates in an investigation, is prohibited and is itself
grounds for termination. This protection applies even if the concern proves
unfounded (Bylaws Appendix B).

**7.5 Conflicts of interest.** Disclose any personal, family, or financial
interest that could affect — or appear to affect — your judgment on the
organization's behalf, before acting. Employees and volunteers with spending,
vendor-selection, or donor-record authority sign the annual conflict disclosure
(Bylaws Appendix A). Do not accept gifts or favors of more than nominal value
from vendors or people seeking to do business with the organization.

**7.6 Outside activity.** Outside work is permitted where it does not conflict
with the organization's interests, does not use its resources or confidential
information, and does not impair performance. Disclose outside work that
involves a vendor, donor, or partner of the organization.

**7.7 Political activity.** The organization takes **no** position for or
against any candidate for public office, on any channel, ever — this is an
absolute condition of its tax exemption. Do not use the organization's name,
marks, platforms, funds, events, or your position within it in connection with a
political campaign. Your own political activity, on your own time, in your own
name, at your own expense, is your own business.

**7.8 Substance-free workplace.** Reporting to work or to an event under the
influence of alcohol or illegal drugs, or using them during work or at
organization events, is prohibited. Lawfully prescribed medication is permitted;
tell your supervisor if it may affect your ability to work safely. Where alcohol
is lawfully served at an organization event, it must be approved in advance and
managed under the event plan.

**7.9 Social media and public statements.** Only people authorized to speak for
the organization do so. Do not post confidential information, donor or attendee
information, unpublished financials, or identifiable images of minors. Personal
accounts should make clear that personal views are personal. Nothing in this
section restricts legally protected activity, including discussion of wages and
working conditions.

**7.10 Attendance.** Be where you said you would be, on time, or tell someone
early. For events, "on time" means the call time in the run of show.

**7.11 Dress.** Dress appropriately for the setting; event roles follow the
event plan.

## 8 · Confidentiality, property, and technology

**8.1 Confidential information.** Donor identities and giving records, personnel
and compensation information, unpublished financials, legal advice, security
plans, and attendee data are confidential. Use them only for the organization's
work; do not disclose them, during or after your service, except as the work
requires or the law compels.

**8.2 Access follows the seat.** Systems access reflects the powers of the seat
you hold (Operating Manual § 3). Do not use someone else's credentials, share
your own, or work around a permission you were not given. If you need access you
do not have, ask for it.

**8.3 Data stays in organization systems.** Organization work happens in
organization accounts. Do not move donor, financial, or attendee data into a
personal account, a personal device's storage, or a personal spreadsheet.
Records may leave the system as a file only through the `data.export` power, for
the organization's own purposes.

**8.4 Property.** Equipment, instruments, cards, devices, and accounts are the
organization's. Take care of them, use them for the work, and return everything
on your last day.

**8.5 Ownership of work.** Everything created within the scope of your work or
service for the organization — content, footage, music, designs, code,
documents, and the accounts holding them — belongs to the organization
(Bylaws § 13.4). Where a work-for-hire or assignment agreement is needed to make
that true, contractors and employees sign one.

**8.6 Monitoring.** The organization may access and review its own systems,
accounts, devices, and records. Do not treat organization systems as private.

**8.7 Security.** Use the organization's password manager and multi-factor
authentication where offered. Report a lost device, a suspected phishing
message, or any suspected breach of donor or financial data **immediately** to
the Executive Director.

## 9 · Safety, events, and protection of minors

**9.1 General safety.** Work safely, follow event safety plans, and report
unsafe conditions. Report any injury, accident, or property damage to your
supervisor the same day, however minor. Do not perform work you have not been
trained to do safely — including lifting, rigging, and electrical work at events.

**9.2 Emergencies at events.** Every event has an assigned person with
first-aid responsibility, a stated emergency plan, and a communications channel.
Call 911 first in an emergency and notify leadership immediately after.

**9.3 Protection of minors and vulnerable adults.** Public Worship gathers in
public places where children are present.

- **Screening.** Anyone in a role with regular contact with minors or vulnerable
  adults, or with unsupervised access to them, must complete a background check
  and an application before beginning, and be re-screened periodically.
- **The two-adult rule.** No adult is ever alone and unobserved with a minor who
  is not their own child. Meetings and conversations happen in view of others.
- **Transportation.** Do not transport a minor alone. Transportation requires a
  guardian's written permission and a second adult.
- **Communication.** Communication with a minor happens through or with the
  knowledge of a guardian, on organization channels, never in private one-to-one
  messages.
- **Photography.** Do not photograph, film, name, or publish an identifiable
  minor without a guardian's written consent.
- **Physical contact.** Appropriate, brief, in view of others, and never in
  response to a child's distress in a way that isolates them.

**9.4 Mandatory reporting.** **Maryland law requires any person** who has reason
to believe a child has been subjected to abuse or neglect to report it to the
local department of social services or law enforcement. This duty is personal
and immediate: it belongs to you, it is not discharged by telling a supervisor,
and it is not subject to internal review or approval first. After reporting,
notify the Executive Director so the organization can meet its own obligations
and cooperate. The same reporting is expected for suspected abuse or neglect of
a vulnerable adult.

**9.5 No retaliation for reporting.** Nobody will face any adverse consequence
for making a report under § 9.4 in good faith.

**9.6 Permits, venues, and insurance.** No event proceeds without the permit or
written venue permission it requires, and without any insurance certificate the
venue requires. This is the Event Lead's responsibility and the Chapter
Director's to confirm.

## 10 · Time off and leave *(employees)*

> **Counsel flag — read before relying on this section.** Most leave
> entitlements depend on employee headcount and on statutes that change. The
> figures below reflect the general Maryland framework and **must be confirmed
> against current law and the organization's actual headcount** before this
> handbook is issued. In particular: Maryland's paid family and medical leave
> insurance program (FAMLI) has had its contribution and benefit start dates
> changed more than once by the legislature — confirm the current dates with
> counsel or the Maryland Department of Labor before the first payroll.

**10.1 Earned sick and safe leave.** Under the Maryland Healthy Working Families
Act, employees accrue sick and safe leave at one (1) hour for every thirty (30)
hours worked, up to forty (40) hours per year. Leave is **paid** where the
organization employs 15 or more employees and **unpaid** where it employs fewer.
It may be used for the employee's own or a family member's illness, preventive
care, or for absences related to domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking.
Leave may be carried over as the statute provides. Notify your supervisor as
soon as practicable; for foreseeable leave, at least seven days in advance.

**10.2 Family and medical leave.** The federal FMLA applies to employers with 50
or more employees and does **not** currently apply to this organization. The
Maryland Parental Leave Act provides up to six (6) weeks of unpaid parental
leave at employers with 15–49 employees. The organization will comply with each
as it becomes applicable.

**10.3 Flexible leave.** Under the Maryland Flexible Leave Act, where the
organization employs 15 or more employees, an employee may use accrued paid
leave for the illness of an immediate family member.

**10.4 Holidays and paid time off.** Paid holidays and any paid time off are set
by the Executive Director within the approved budget, stated in the offer letter,
and published to staff. *(To be filled in on adoption — see Open decisions.)*

**10.5 Bereavement leave.** Available on request; the amount is arranged with
your supervisor.

**10.6 Jury duty and court appearances.** Time off is provided for jury service
as required by law. No one will be penalized for serving. Bring the summons to
your supervisor as soon as you receive it.

**10.7 Voting leave.** Maryland provides employees time off to vote where they
do not otherwise have sufficient time outside working hours, as the Election Law
provides.

**10.8 Military leave.** Provided in accordance with USERRA and Maryland law,
with reinstatement rights as those laws require.

**10.9 Requesting time off.** Request in advance through your supervisor. For
event-critical roles, coordinate coverage before the request is approved.

**10.10 Volunteers.** Volunteers have no leave entitlement — they have a
commitment they set. Tell your lead early when you cannot make something.

## 11 · Raising a concern

Use whichever route fits, and use it early.

1. **Your supervisor** — the fastest route for most things.
2. **The Executive Director** — where the supervisor is the problem, or the
   matter is serious.
3. **The Chair of the Board, or the Treasurer** — where the concern involves the
   Executive Director, involves money, or you are not comfortable reporting
   internally. Contact routes are published to staff and volunteers and kept
   current.

**Anonymous reports are accepted** and will be investigated as far as the
information allows. Reports of financial wrongdoing, illegality, or harm to a
person are reported to the Board (Bylaws Appendix B). **No retaliation. Ever.**

## 12 · Performance, discipline, and separation *(employees)*

**12.1 Feedback.** Supervisors give feedback continuously and hold a documented
review at least annually. The Executive Director is evaluated annually by the
Board.

**12.2 Discipline.** Where a problem is correctable, the organization generally
uses progressive steps — conversation, written warning, final warning,
termination — but is not required to, and may move directly to termination
where the conduct warrants it. Nothing here alters at-will employment.

**12.3 Conduct that may result in immediate termination.** Theft or misuse of
funds or property; falsifying records, time, or receipts; violence or threats;
harassment; breach of donor or personal data; violation of the child-protection
rules; being under the influence at work or at an event; and retaliation against
someone who raised a concern.

**12.4 Resignation.** Two weeks' notice is requested. The organization may
accept a resignation effective immediately.

**12.5 On the last day.** Return all property, cards, keys, and equipment;
transfer any account or record held personally into the organization's control;
and complete handover of your seat's work. Access and powers are removed the
same day (Operating Manual § 12.1). Final wages are paid as § 6.8 provides.

**12.6 References.** Reference requests are directed to the Executive Director,
who confirms dates of service and position held.

## Open decisions

1. **Are there employees yet, and how many?** Nearly every legal threshold in
   §§ 7 and 10 turns on headcount. The handbook cannot be issued accurately
   without this answer.
2. **Paid time off and holidays** (§ 10.4) — the schedule, the accrual, whether
   PTO is banked or unlimited, and whether unused PTO is paid out on separation
   (which is a legal question in Maryland, not just a policy choice).
3. **Pay practices** — pay frequency, whether any position is exempt, and the
   compensation philosophy the Board wants for the Executive Director role
   (Bylaws § 12.6).
4. **Statement of faith** (§ 3.3) — whether one exists, whether employees are
   required to subscribe to it, and which roles if any are ministerial. Do not
   apply any faith-based requirement before counsel reviews it.
5. **Background-check provider and scope** (§ 9.3), re-screening interval, and
   which roles are in scope. This should be settled before the next event
   season, not at adoption.
6. **Child-protection policy depth** — § 9.3 is a summary. An organization
   gathering publicly where children are present should adopt a standalone
   child-protection policy with training, and confirm that its insurance
   includes abuse and molestation coverage.
7. **Multi-state workers.** The moment a worker lives outside Maryland, that
   state's wage, leave, and registration rules apply to them. Get a state
   supplement before hiring across a state line.
8. **Workers' compensation** — confirm the policy is in force before the first
   employee starts, and confirm what coverage, if any, volunteers have.
9. **Whether volunteers sign an agreement** covering conduct, confidentiality,
   child protection, and ownership of work (§ 8.5). Recommended: yes, for every
   screened role.
