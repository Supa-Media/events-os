# BYLAWS OF GLOBAL ECHO CHARITABLE ORGANIZATION

*A Maryland nonstock corporation operating as **Public Worship***

---

## ARTICLE I — NAME, OFFICES, AND PURPOSES

**1.1 Name.** The name of the corporation is Global Echo Charitable
Organization (the "Corporation").

**1.2 Assumed names.** The Corporation conducts its charitable and religious
programs publicly under the name **Public Worship**, and may adopt other trade
names by resolution of the Board of Directors (the "Board"). The Board shall
cause each assumed name to be registered where required, and shall keep the
register of assumed names with the corporate records. Contracts, gift receipts,
and filings are executed in the Corporation's legal name, with the trade name
shown as "d/b/a" where useful.

**1.3 Principal office.** The Corporation's principal office is located in the
State of Maryland at the address most recently designated by the Board. The
Corporation may have other offices, within or outside Maryland, as the Board
designates or the affairs of the Corporation require. The Corporation shall
continuously maintain a resident agent and registered office in Maryland as
required by law.

**1.4 Purposes.** The Corporation is organized and shall be operated
exclusively for religious, charitable, and educational purposes within the
meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended
(the "Code"), or the corresponding section of any future federal tax code.
Within those purposes, the Corporation exists to create holy experiences
through music that ignite faith in Jesus Christ; to gather people in public
places for genuine worship; to serve the neighborhoods in which it gathers; and
to equip and support local chapters that carry this work into their own cities.

**1.5 Nonprofit and religious character.** The Corporation is a nonstock
corporation formed under the Maryland General Corporation Law as applied to
nonstock corporations (Md. Code Ann., Corps. & Ass'ns, Title 5, Subtitle 2). It
is a religious organization. Its governing documents, employment standards, and
program decisions may reflect its religious character to the extent permitted by
law, including Section 702 of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

**1.6 Powers.** Subject to these Bylaws, the Articles of Incorporation, and
applicable law, the Corporation may exercise all powers conferred on Maryland
nonstock corporations in furtherance of its purposes.

## ARTICLE II — TAX EXEMPTION AND LIMITATIONS ON ACTIVITIES

**2.1 Exempt operation.** No part of the Corporation's activities shall consist
of activities not permitted to be carried on by an organization exempt from
federal income tax under Section 501(c)(3) of the Code, or by an organization
contributions to which are deductible under Section 170(c)(2) of the Code.

**2.2 Private inurement prohibited.** No part of the net earnings of the
Corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its
directors, officers, or other private persons, except that the Corporation may
pay reasonable compensation for services actually rendered and may reimburse
expenses actually incurred in furtherance of its purposes, in each case in
accordance with Article XII.

**2.3 Excess benefit transactions.** The Corporation shall not engage in any
transaction that constitutes an excess benefit transaction under Section 4958
of the Code. Any compensation or property transfer to a person in a position to
exercise substantial influence over the Corporation's affairs shall be approved
under the procedure in Section 12.6.

**2.4 Lobbying.** No substantial part of the Corporation's activities shall
consist of carrying on propaganda or otherwise attempting to influence
legislation.

**2.5 Political campaigns.** The Corporation shall not participate or intervene
in — including by publishing or distributing statements concerning — any
political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public
office. This prohibition is absolute and applies to every chapter, every
official communication channel, and every use of the Corporation's name, marks,
platforms, funds, or events. It does not restrain what an individual does on
their own time, in their own name, and at their own expense.

**2.6 Private benefit.** The Corporation's activities shall not serve private
interests other than incidentally to the accomplishment of its exempt purposes.

**2.7 Precedence.** Notwithstanding any other provision of these Bylaws, the
Corporation shall not carry on any activity prohibited by this Article. This
Article controls over any conflicting provision of these Bylaws or of any
policy, resolution, or delegation adopted under them.

## ARTICLE III — NO MEMBERS

**3.1 No members.** The Corporation has no members within the meaning of the
Maryland General Corporation Law. All corporate powers are exercised by or
under the authority of the Board.

**3.2 The Team.** People who serve the Corporation — volunteers, chapter
leadership, staff, artists, and musicians, collectively "the Team" — hold no
membership rights, no vote in corporate matters, and no property interest in the
Corporation. Their roles, duties, and authority are defined by the seat they
hold under Article VIII and the Operating Manual, and may be changed or ended as
provided there.

**3.3 Participants.** Attending an event, giving to the Corporation, backing a
chapter, or subscribing to its communications creates no membership,
governance right, or expectation of continued benefit.

## ARTICLE IV — BOARD OF DIRECTORS

**4.1 General powers.** The business and affairs of the Corporation shall be
managed by or under the direction of the Board. The Board is ultimately
responsible for the Corporation's mission, financial integrity, legal
compliance, and the selection, oversight, support, and compensation of the
Executive Director.

**4.2 Number.** The Board shall consist of no fewer than three (3) and no more
than fifteen (15) directors. Within that range, the number is fixed from time
to time by resolution of the Board. No decrease in the number of directors
shortens the term of an incumbent director.

**4.3 Qualifications.** Directors shall be individuals aged eighteen (18) or
older who affirm the Corporation's purposes and are willing to accept the duties
of care, loyalty, and obedience owed to a Maryland charitable corporation.
Directors need not be Maryland residents.

**4.4 Independence.** A majority of directors then in office shall be
**independent**. A director is independent if the director:

  (a) is not, and has not within the prior twelve (12) months been, compensated
      by the Corporation as an employee or contractor;
  (b) does not have a family or business relationship with a person who is; and
  (c) does not receive, directly or indirectly, material financial benefit from
      the Corporation other than reimbursement of expenses and benefits
      available to the general public.

Employed or compensated persons may serve as directors only within this limit,
and shall not vote on their own compensation, evaluation, or continued service
(Section 12.6).

**4.5 Term.** Directors serve three (3)-year terms and may serve no more than
three (3) consecutive full terms, after which a one (1)-year interval is
required before re-election. A director whose term expires continues to serve
until a successor is elected or the seat is eliminated. Terms should be
staggered so that roughly one third expire each year; the Board may allocate
initial terms of one, two, or three years to establish the stagger.

**4.6 Election.** Directors are elected by a majority vote of the directors
then in office, at the annual meeting or at any regular or special meeting for
which notice of the election was given.

**4.7 Resignation.** A director may resign at any time by delivering written
notice (including by electronic transmission) to the Chair or the Secretary. A
resignation takes effect when delivered or at a later time stated in it, and
does not require acceptance.

**4.8 Removal.** A director may be removed, with or without cause, by the
affirmative vote of two thirds (2/3) of the directors then in office, excluding
the director in question, at a meeting for which the removal was stated in the
notice. A director shall be given notice of the proposed removal and an
opportunity to be heard by the Board before the vote.

**4.9 Automatic vacancy.** A seat becomes vacant without further action upon a
director's death, resignation, removal, adjudicated incapacity, or failure to
attend three (3) consecutive regular meetings without an excuse accepted by the
Board.

**4.10 Vacancies.** A vacancy, including one created by an increase in the
authorized number of directors, is filled by a majority vote of the remaining
directors, even if fewer than a quorum. A director so elected serves the
unexpired portion of the term.

**4.11 Compensation.** Directors serve without compensation for their service
as directors. The Corporation may reimburse a director's reasonable expenses of
attending meetings or performing Board duties. Nothing in this Section prevents
a director who is also an employee or contractor from receiving reasonable
compensation for those services, subject to Article XII and the independence
requirement of Section 4.4.

**4.12 No loans.** The Corporation shall make no loan to any director or
officer, and shall not guarantee the obligation of any director or officer.

**4.13 Standard of conduct.** A director shall act in good faith, with the care
an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would use under similar
circumstances, and in a manner the director reasonably believes to be in the
best interests of the Corporation. A director may rely on information, opinions,
reports, and financial statements prepared by officers, employees, counsel,
accountants, or committees the director reasonably believes to be reliable and
competent.

**4.14 Duty of confidentiality.** Directors shall keep confidential all
non-public information learned in their service — personnel matters, donor
identities and giving records, security and safety information, legal advice,
and matters discussed in executive session — and shall not use it for personal
advantage. This duty survives the end of service.

## ARTICLE V — MEETINGS OF THE BOARD

**5.1 Annual meeting.** The Board shall hold an annual meeting to elect
directors and officers, review the Corporation's financial condition and the
prior year's Form 990, receive the Executive Director's report, and conduct
other business.

**5.2 Regular meetings.** The Board shall meet at least **four (4) times per
calendar year**, including the annual meeting. By resolution the Board may fix
the time and place of regular meetings, in which case no further notice is
required.

**5.3 Special meetings.** Special meetings may be called by the Chair, the
Executive Director, or any two (2) directors, at any place or by any means of
remote communication designated in the call.

**5.4 Notice.** Notice of a special meeting stating the date, time, place (or
means of remote participation), and — for matters requiring it under these
Bylaws — the business to be transacted, shall be delivered to each director at
least **five (5) days** before the meeting, by personal delivery, mail, email,
or other electronic transmission to the address most recently furnished by the
director. Notice by mail is effective when deposited, properly addressed with
postage prepaid; electronic notice is effective when transmitted. A shorter
period, not less than twenty-four (24) hours, is permitted where the Chair
determines that the matter is urgent and states the reason in the notice.
Except where these Bylaws require the purpose to be stated, neither the business
to be transacted nor the purpose of a meeting need be specified in the notice.

**5.5 Waiver of notice.** A director may waive notice before or after the
meeting by a signed writing (including electronic signature) delivered to the
Secretary. Attendance at a meeting waives notice, except where the director
attends for the express purpose of objecting to the transaction of business
because the meeting was not lawfully called or convened, and does not thereafter
vote for or assent to action taken.

**5.6 Quorum.** A majority of the directors then in office constitutes a
quorum. If a quorum is not present, a majority of directors present may adjourn
the meeting without further notice. A director's participation for the purpose
of objecting to the meeting's convening does not count toward a quorum.

**5.7 Manner of acting.** The act of a majority of directors present at a
meeting at which a quorum is present is the act of the Board, unless a greater
number is required by law, the Articles of Incorporation, or these Bylaws.

**5.8 Remote participation.** Directors may participate by conference
telephone, video conference, or any other means by which all participants can
hear and be heard by one another at the same time. Participation by such means
constitutes presence in person. The Secretary shall record the means used and
who participated remotely.

**5.9 Action without a meeting.** Any action required or permitted to be taken
at a Board meeting may be taken without a meeting if **all** directors then in
office consent in writing, including by electronic transmission bearing an
electronic signature. The consents shall state the action taken, be filed with
the minutes, and have the effect of a unanimous vote. This method shall not be
used for the election or removal of a director or officer, the approval of
compensation for the Executive Director, or the amendment of these Bylaws.

**5.10 Presumption of assent.** A director present at a meeting at which action
is taken is presumed to have assented unless the director's dissent or
abstention is entered in the minutes, or the director delivers a written dissent
to the Secretary before adjournment or promptly after. A director who voted in
favor may not dissent afterward.

**5.11 Executive session.** The Board may meet in executive session, with only
directors present, to consider personnel matters, compensation, legal advice,
matters of individual privacy, or any matter the Board determines requires
confidentiality. Only directors vote in executive session. The existence of the
session and any action taken shall be recorded in the minutes.

**5.12 Minutes.** The Secretary shall keep minutes of every meeting recording
the date, time, means, directors present (including remotely), matters
considered, actions taken, the vote count on each action, recusals, and
conflicts disclosed. Minutes are approved at a subsequent meeting and retained
permanently.

**5.13 Guests.** The Chair may invite staff, advisors, or guests to attend all
or part of a meeting. Guests have no vote and do not count toward a quorum.

## ARTICLE VI — OFFICERS

**6.1 Officers.** The officers of the Corporation are a **Chair**, a
**Secretary**, and a **Treasurer**, each elected by and from the Board. The
Board may also elect a **Vice Chair** and appoint such assistant officers as it
deems useful. Officers serve as officers without compensation.

**6.2 One person, two offices.** One person may hold two or more offices,
except that the same person shall not hold the offices of Chair and Secretary.

**6.3 Election and term.** Officers are elected annually and serve one (1)-year
terms until a successor is elected, or until earlier death, resignation, or
removal. An officer must be a director.

**6.4 Resignation and removal.** An officer may resign at any time by written
notice to the Chair or the Secretary. The Board may remove any officer, with or
without cause, by majority vote of the directors then in office. Removal from
office does not by itself remove the person as a director.

**6.5 Vacancies.** The Board fills a vacancy in any office for the unexpired
portion of the term.

**6.6 Chair.** The Chair presides at all Board meetings, sets the agenda in
consultation with the Executive Director, serves as the Board's principal link
to the Executive Director, leads the annual evaluation of the Executive
Director, and performs such other duties as the Board assigns. The Chair is not
the Corporation's chief executive; the Executive Director is (Article VIII).

**6.7 Vice Chair.** The Vice Chair, if any, performs the Chair's duties in the
Chair's absence or inability to act, and such other duties as the Board or Chair
assigns.

**6.8 Secretary.** The Secretary shall: (a) keep the minutes of Board and
committee meetings; (b) see that notices are duly given; (c) act as custodian of
the corporate records and, where one is used, the corporate seal; (d) maintain
the register of directors' addresses, the register of assumed names, and the
records of conflict-of-interest disclosures; (e) certify corporate records and
resolutions; (f) see that required annual filings are made; and (g) perform such
other duties as the Board or Chair assigns. The Secretary may delegate the
mechanics of these duties to staff but remains accountable for them.

**6.9 Treasurer.** The Treasurer shall: (a) oversee the Corporation's financial
affairs on behalf of the Board; (b) see that full and accurate accounts are
kept; (c) present financial statements to the Board at each regular meeting and
an annual financial report at the annual meeting; (d) chair the Finance and
Audit Committee if one exists; (e) review the annual Form 990 before filing and
present it to the Board; and (f) perform such other duties as the Board assigns.
Day-to-day custody of funds, bookkeeping, and disbursement are performed by
staff under Article VIII and the Operating Manual; the Treasurer's role is
oversight, not operation. The Board may require the Treasurer or any person
with custody of funds to be bonded at the Corporation's expense.

**6.10 The corporate officer titles are not the org chart.** The offices in
this Article are governance offices. The Corporation's operating seats —
Executive Director, Financial Manager, Chapter Director, chapter Treasurer, and
the rest — are defined under Article VIII and in the Operating Manual, and a
title appearing in both places does not merge the two roles. In particular, the
**chapter Treasurer** seat is an operating seat and is not the corporate
Treasurer.

## ARTICLE VII — COMMITTEES

**7.1 Establishment.** The Board may by resolution of a majority of directors
then in office establish standing or ad hoc committees, define their charters,
and appoint their members. Each committee reports to the Board and keeps minutes
of its meetings.

**7.2 Board committees and advisory committees.** A committee that exercises
delegated authority of the Board (a "Board committee") shall consist solely of
two (2) or more directors. A committee that only advises, recommends, or does
staff-supporting work may include non-directors, and exercises no authority of
the Board.

**7.3 Limits on delegated authority.** No committee may: amend the Articles of
Incorporation or these Bylaws; elect or remove directors or officers; approve a
merger, sale of substantially all assets, or dissolution; adopt the annual
budget; approve the Executive Director's compensation; fill Board vacancies; or
take any action these Bylaws reserve to the full Board. Delegation never
relieves the Board or any director of the duties in Section 4.13.

**7.4 Executive Committee.** The Board may elect an Executive Committee of no
fewer than three (3) directors, including the Chair. Between meetings of the
Board, the Executive Committee may act on matters that cannot reasonably await
the next Board meeting, within the limits of Section 7.3 and any dollar or
subject-matter limits the Board sets by resolution. **Every action of the
Executive Committee shall be reported to the full Board at or before its next
meeting and recorded in the Board's minutes.** The Executive Committee is
chaired by the Chair of the Board. A majority of its members is a quorum;
notice of twenty-four (24) hours, given orally or in writing, is sufficient; and
it may act without a meeting only by unanimous written consent of all its
members.

**7.5 Finance and Audit Committee.** The Board should maintain a Finance and
Audit Committee, chaired by the Treasurer, to review financial statements and
internal controls, oversee the budget's preparation for Board approval, oversee
any independent audit, review, or compilation, review the Form 990 before
filing, and review the Corporation's investment and reserve practices. A
majority of its members shall be independent under Section 4.4, and no employee
shall chair it.

**7.6 Governance and Nominating Committee.** The Board should maintain a
Governance and Nominating Committee to recruit and nominate directors, oversee
director orientation and evaluation, administer the conflict-of-interest
disclosure process, and review these Bylaws and the Corporation's governance
policies at least annually.

**7.7 Meetings.** Except as a committee's charter or this Article provides, the
notice, quorum, remote-participation, and action-without-meeting rules of
Article V apply to committees.

## ARTICLE VIII — EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR, STAFF, AND THE SEAT CHART

**8.1 Executive Director.** The Board shall appoint an **Executive Director**
as the Corporation's chief executive. The Executive Director is accountable to
the Board as a whole, is evaluated annually by the Board, and serves at the
Board's pleasure. The Executive Director may be a paid employee; the position's
compensation is set under Section 12.6.

**8.2 Authority.** Subject to the Board's oversight, the policies it adopts,
the approved budget, and these Bylaws, the Executive Director shall: manage the
Corporation's day-to-day operations, programs, and staff; hire, supervise,
compensate within budget, and terminate employees and contractors; execute
contracts within the limits of Section 10.1; represent the Corporation
publicly; and implement the Board's direction. The Executive Director shall
keep the Board informed, promptly and candidly, of any matter material to the
Corporation's mission, finances, legal exposure, or reputation.

**8.3 Absence or vacancy.** The Board shall maintain a written succession plan
naming who exercises the Executive Director's authority during an absence and
what happens on a vacancy. Where no plan is in force, the Chair exercises that
authority until the Board acts.

**8.4 The seat chart.** The Corporation's operating roles are organized as a
**seat chart** — a central chart for the organization and a chapter chart for
each chapter — maintained in the Corporation's software of record. Each seat
carries duties and a set of named **powers**. The Executive Director maintains
the seat chart and appoints and removes seat holders, except that:

  (a) the Board appoints and removes the Executive Director;
  (b) a seat carrying authority to approve spending, publish the Corporation's
      books, or export donor records is filled only with the Executive
      Director's express approval; and
  (c) the Board may by resolution reserve any seat or power to itself.

**8.5 Powers are named, granted, and revocable.** Authority to act on the
Corporation's behalf is exercised only through a named power granted to a seat.
No person exercises operational authority by title, seniority, tenure, or
custom. The Executive Director may grant, narrow, or revoke a seat's powers at
any time, and shall do so in the software of record so that the seat chart
remains the accurate answer to who may do what.

**8.6 Separation of duties.** Where a power approves, the approver shall not be
the person who submitted, requested, or would benefit from the matter. The
Corporation shall maintain, in the Operating Manual, the specific separations it
requires — including approval of spending, reimbursements, contractor payments,
and mass communications — and shall enforce them in software where the software
supports it.

**8.7 Volunteers.** Most of the Corporation's work is performed by volunteers,
including nearly all chapter leadership. Volunteers serve at will, receive no
compensation, and may be given only reimbursement of actual expenses and
non-cash recognition of nominal value. The Corporation shall not accept
volunteer service from an employee in the same type of work the employee is
paid to perform.

**8.8 Staff policies.** The Board shall adopt an employee handbook and such
personnel policies as it deems appropriate; the Executive Director administers
them. In case of conflict, these Bylaws control over the handbook.

## ARTICLE IX — CHAPTERS

**9.1 Chapters are the Corporation.** A **chapter** is a local expression of
the Corporation's programs in a city or region. A chapter is **not** a separate
legal entity, has no independent legal existence, and may not incorporate,
obtain its own employer identification number, apply for its own tax exemption,
open its own bank accounts, hold its own insurance, or hold assets in its own
name. Every chapter's activities are the Corporation's activities, every
chapter's obligations are the Corporation's obligations, and every dollar a
chapter raises or holds is the Corporation's money.

**9.2 Establishment and closure.** The Board authorizes the establishment of
chapters and delegates to the Executive Director the decision to launch, pause,
merge, or close a specific chapter under criteria the Board approves. On
closure, the chapter's funds, equipment, records, accounts, and intellectual
property remain with the Corporation.

**9.3 Chapter leadership.** Each chapter is led by a **Chapter Director**
appointed by and accountable to the Executive Director, supported by the seats
defined in the chapter chart. Chapter leadership is volunteer service unless the
Board approves otherwise.

**9.4 Chapter finances.** A chapter operates against a budget approved under
the Corporation's financial policies, spends through the Corporation's payment
instruments, and is bookkept in the Corporation's system of record. Chapters
share in the cost of the Corporation's central work and in the funding of new
city launches on the basis set out in the Operating Manual, as approved by the
Board.

**9.5 Restricted and designated gifts.** A gift solicited for a named chapter
or purpose shall be used for that chapter or purpose. Where a gift's stated
purpose becomes impracticable, the Corporation shall seek the donor's consent to
redirect it and, failing that, shall follow applicable Maryland law. The Board
retains ultimate authority and discretion over the use of all funds consistent
with the Corporation's purposes.

**9.6 Name, marks, and channels.** Chapters use the Corporation's names, marks,
brand, domains, and communication channels under license from the Corporation,
revocable at will, and only in accordance with the Corporation's brand and
communications policies. No chapter may register a domain, trade name, social
account, or payment account bearing the Corporation's names or marks except in
the Corporation's name and under its control.

**9.7 Compliance.** Before a chapter solicits contributions or holds events in
a jurisdiction, the Executive Director shall confirm that the Corporation's
charitable-solicitation registration, insurance, permits, and any other required
authorizations cover that jurisdiction and activity.

**9.8 Standards and discipline.** Chapters shall operate in accordance with
these Bylaws, the Corporation's policies, and the Operating Manual. The
Executive Director may suspend a chapter's activities, spending authority, or
leadership appointments for cause, and shall report any suspension to the Board
at or before its next meeting.

## ARTICLE X — FINANCIAL ADMINISTRATION

**10.1 Contracts.** The Board may authorize any officer, employee, or agent to
enter into contracts and execute instruments in the Corporation's name; the
authority may be general or confined to specific instances. Absent a contrary
resolution, the Executive Director may execute contracts within the approved
budget. The following require prior Board approval: any contract or series of
related contracts exceeding the threshold the Board sets by resolution; any
borrowing, guarantee, or encumbrance of corporate assets; any lease or purchase
of real property; any merger, consolidation, sale of substantially all assets,
or dissolution; and any transaction with an interested person under Article XII.

**10.2 Loans and indebtedness.** No loan shall be contracted on behalf of the
Corporation, and no evidence of indebtedness issued in its name, without a
Board resolution. No loans to directors or officers (Section 4.12).

**10.3 Checks, drafts, and payment instruments.** All checks, drafts, transfers,
card issuances, and other orders for the payment of money shall be signed or
authorized by the persons and in the manner the Board determines by resolution.
The Board shall establish dual-authorization requirements above thresholds it
sets, and the Executive Director shall implement them in the Corporation's
banking and payment systems.

**10.4 Deposits and custody.** All funds shall be deposited to the
Corporation's credit in banks, trust companies, or other depositories selected
by the Board. Corporate funds shall never be held in a personal account, and
personal funds shall never be commingled with corporate funds.

**10.5 Acceptance of gifts.** The Corporation may accept gifts, grants,
bequests, and devises consistent with its purposes, subject to the Gift
Acceptance Policy at **Appendix D**. Acceptance of a gift with unusual
conditions, of illiquid or non-cash property, or that would obligate the
Corporation to future expenditure requires the Executive Director's approval and,
above the thresholds in Appendix D, the Board's.

**10.6 Fiscal year.** The fiscal year is the calendar year, unless a different
fiscal year is adopted for federal tax purposes, in which case it is the year so
adopted.

**10.7 Budget.** The Executive Director shall present a proposed annual
operating budget to the Board before the start of each fiscal year, and the
Board shall adopt it. The Executive Director shall report actual results against
budget at each regular Board meeting, and shall obtain Board approval for
material variances as defined by resolution.

**10.8 Reserves.** The Board should adopt and periodically review an operating
reserve target.

**10.9 Financial review.** The Board shall obtain the level of independent
financial examination appropriate to the Corporation's size and to the
requirements of Maryland charitable-solicitation law and any funder — an
independent audit, review, or compilation — and shall review the results
directly with the accountant, without staff present for at least part of that
discussion.

**10.10 Internal controls.** The Board shall adopt written internal controls
addressing at least: segregation of duties between recording, approving, and
disbursing; documentation and substantiation of every expenditure; timely
reconciliation of accounts; approval of reimbursements and contractor payments
by someone other than the payee; and periodic review of access and payment
instruments. Controls implemented in the Corporation's software satisfy this
Section to the extent they are actually enforced there.

**10.11 Annual filings.** The Corporation shall timely file its federal Form
990-series return, its Maryland annual charitable-organization registration and
any other state charitable registrations required where it solicits, its
Maryland annual report and personal property return, and all payroll and
information returns. The Board shall be furnished a copy of the Form 990 before
it is filed.

## ARTICLE XI — TRANSPARENCY AND PUBLIC REPORTING

**11.1 The commitment.** The Corporation publishes its books. It shall publish,
on a public page, a periodic accounting of the money it receives and spends, at
a level of detail sufficient for a member of the public to see what the
Corporation's money actually did.

**11.2 What is published is what was approved.** A published period is
**frozen** at the moment of publication. The public record is that frozen copy,
not a live view of the Corporation's books, so that no later edit can silently
rewrite what the public was told.

**11.3 Corrections are published, not made quietly.** An error in a published
period is corrected by publishing a further revision of that period, stating the
reason for the correction, with the prior revision remaining publicly readable
beside it.

**11.4 Personal information.** Publication shall omit information whose
disclosure would be unlawful, would endanger a person, or would identify a
donor, an employee's compensation other than as required by law, or a minor.
The obligation to publish never overrides the obligation to protect people.

**11.5 Who publishes.** Authority to publish a period is a named power held by
seats designated in the Operating Manual, and shall not be held by the same
person who prepared the period.

**11.6 Records available on request.** The Corporation shall make available for
public inspection its application for exemption, its determination letter, and
its three most recent Form 990-series returns, as required by law.

## ARTICLE XII — CONFLICTS OF INTEREST AND COMPENSATION

**12.1 Policy.** The Corporation shall maintain and follow the Conflict of
Interest Policy at **Appendix A**, which is incorporated into these Bylaws.

**12.2 Who is covered.** Directors, officers, committee members with Board-
delegated powers, the Executive Director, and any employee or volunteer with
authority to spend, approve spending, select vendors, or access donor records.

**12.3 Annual disclosure.** Each covered person shall sign the Appendix A
disclosure statement upon appointment and annually thereafter, and shall
promptly disclose any interest arising during the year. The Secretary retains the
statements.

**12.4 Procedure.** An interested person shall disclose the interest and the
material facts, may present information, and shall then leave the meeting for
the deliberation and vote. The disinterested directors shall determine whether a
more advantageous arrangement is reasonably attainable, and may approve the
transaction only if a majority of them find it fair and reasonable to the
Corporation and in its best interests. The disclosure, the recusal, the finding,
and the vote shall be recorded in the minutes.

**12.5 Related-party transactions.** Any transaction between the Corporation
and a director, officer, the Executive Director, a family member of any of them,
or an entity in which any of them has a material financial interest requires
prior Board approval under Section 12.4, regardless of amount, and shall be
tracked for disclosure on the Form 990.

**12.6 Compensation.** Compensation of the Executive Director, of any officer or
director, and of any other person in a position to exercise substantial
influence over the Corporation's affairs shall be approved **in advance** by the
Board or a Board committee composed entirely of persons with no conflict of
interest with respect to the arrangement, relying on appropriate data as to
comparability, and documented contemporaneously in minutes recording the terms
approved, the date, who voted, the comparability data relied on, and any
recusal. This procedure is intended to satisfy the rebuttable-presumption
requirements of Treasury Regulation § 53.4958-6. No person shall participate in
a decision concerning their own compensation.

**12.7 Gifts and inducements.** No covered person shall accept a gift, favor, or
inducement of more than nominal value from a vendor, grantee, contractor, or
person seeking to do business with the Corporation.

## ARTICLE XIII — BOOKS AND RECORDS

**13.1 Records maintained.** The Corporation shall keep at its principal
office, or at such other place as the Board designates: correct and complete
books and records of account; minutes of Board and committee proceedings; the
Articles of Incorporation and these Bylaws as amended; the register of
directors; conflict-of-interest disclosures; the Corporation's exemption
application, determination letter, and filed returns; and such other records as
law requires or the Board directs. Records maintained in the Corporation's
software of record satisfy this Section.

**13.2 Director inspection.** A director may inspect any book, record, or
facility of the Corporation for a purpose reasonably related to the director's
duties, at a reasonable time and on reasonable notice to the Secretary.

**13.3 Retention.** The Corporation shall follow the Document Retention and
Destruction Policy at **Appendix C**. No document shall be altered or destroyed
when litigation, an investigation, or an audit is pending or reasonably
anticipated.

**13.4 Ownership of work product and accounts.** All records, data, donor
information, content, accounts, domains, and intellectual property created by or
for the Corporation, including by volunteers and chapters, are the property of
the Corporation, and shall be maintained in the Corporation's systems and under
its control.

## ARTICLE XIV — INDEMNIFICATION AND INSURANCE

**14.1 Indemnification.** To the fullest extent permitted by the Maryland
General Corporation Law (Md. Code Ann., Corps. & Ass'ns § 2-418) as applied to
nonstock corporations, the Corporation shall indemnify any person who is or was
a director or officer of the Corporation, and who is or was a party to or
threatened to be made a party to any threatened, pending, or completed civil,
criminal, administrative, or investigative action, suit, or proceeding by reason
of that service, or by reason of serving at the Corporation's request as a
director, officer, employee, or agent of another entity, against judgments,
penalties, fines, settlements, and reasonable expenses (including attorneys'
fees) actually incurred.

**14.2 Limits.** Indemnification is not available where it is established that
the act or omission of the person was material to the matter giving rise to the
proceeding and was committed in bad faith or was the result of active and
deliberate dishonesty, where the person actually received an improper personal
benefit in money, property, or services, or, in a criminal proceeding, where the
person had reasonable cause to believe the act or omission was unlawful.

**14.3 Advancement of expenses.** The Corporation may advance reasonable
expenses to a director or officer in advance of final disposition upon receipt
of a written affirmation of good faith belief that the standard of conduct has
been met and a written undertaking to repay the amount if it is ultimately
determined that the standard was not met.

**14.4 Others.** The Board may, in its discretion, indemnify employees, agents,
and volunteers of the Corporation to the same extent.

**14.5 Non-exclusive.** The indemnification provided by this Article is not
exclusive of other rights to which a person may be entitled by law, agreement,
or resolution.

**14.6 Insurance.** The Corporation shall maintain directors and officers
liability insurance, general liability insurance appropriate to public events,
and such other coverage — including workers' compensation where required,
abuse and molestation coverage where minors or vulnerable adults are served, and
coverage for equipment and volunteers — as the Board determines prudent.

## ARTICLE XV — AMENDMENTS

**15.1 Amendment.** These Bylaws may be amended, restated, or repealed and new
bylaws adopted by the affirmative vote of **two thirds (2/3) of the directors
then in office**, at any regular or special meeting for which the text or
substance of the proposed change was included in the notice, delivered at least
five (5) days in advance.

**15.2 Limits.** No amendment may authorize conduct inconsistent with Article II
or Article XVI, or with the Articles of Incorporation. If these Bylaws and the
Articles of Incorporation conflict, the Articles control.

**15.3 Record.** The Secretary shall record each amendment with its adoption
date and maintain a current, dated copy of these Bylaws as the operative
version.

## ARTICLE XVI — DISSOLUTION

**16.1 Dissolution.** The Corporation may be dissolved only by the affirmative
vote of two thirds (2/3) of the directors then in office, following the
procedure required by Maryland law.

**16.2 Distribution of assets.** Upon dissolution, after paying or making
provision for all liabilities, the Board shall dispose of all remaining assets
exclusively for the purposes of the Corporation, by distributing them to one or
more organizations then qualifying as exempt under Section 501(c)(3) of the Code
and organized and operated exclusively for religious, charitable, or educational
purposes, as the Board determines. Any assets not so disposed of shall be
disposed of by a court of competent jurisdiction in the county in which the
Corporation's principal office is then located, exclusively for such purposes or
to such organizations as that court determines. No assets shall be distributed
to any director, officer, employee, or private individual.

**16.3 Restricted funds.** Assets held subject to a donor restriction shall be
distributed consistent with that restriction.

# APPENDIX A — CONFLICT OF INTEREST POLICY

**A.1 Purpose.** To protect the Corporation's interest when it contemplates a
transaction that might benefit the private interest of a covered person, or
result in an excess benefit transaction, and to supplement — not replace — any
applicable state or federal law.

**A.2 Covered persons.** As defined in Section 12.2 of the Bylaws.

**A.3 Financial interest.** A person has a financial interest if the person
has, directly or through a family member or a business in which they hold an
interest, (a) an ownership or investment interest in any entity with which the
Corporation has or is negotiating a transaction or arrangement, (b) a
compensation arrangement with the Corporation or with any such entity, or (c) a
potential ownership, investment, or compensation interest in any entity or
person with which the Corporation is negotiating. Compensation includes direct
and indirect remuneration and gifts or favors of more than nominal value. A
financial interest is not necessarily a conflict — a conflict exists when the
Board or committee determines one does.

**A.4 Duty to disclose.** A covered person shall disclose the existence of a
financial interest and all material facts to the directors or committee members
considering the transaction, as soon as the person becomes aware of it.

**A.5 Determining whether a conflict exists.** After disclosure and any
response to questions, the interested person shall leave the meeting while the
determination is discussed and voted upon. The remaining members decide whether
a conflict of interest exists.

**A.6 Procedure for addressing a conflict.** The chair of the meeting may
appoint a disinterested person or committee to investigate alternatives. After
exercising due diligence, the Board or committee shall determine whether the
Corporation can obtain a more advantageous transaction with reasonable efforts
from a person or entity that would not give rise to a conflict. If not, it shall
determine by a majority vote of the disinterested members whether the
transaction is in the Corporation's best interest, is fair and reasonable, and
should be approved.

**A.7 Violations.** If the Board or committee has reasonable cause to believe a
covered person has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict, it shall
inform the person of the basis and afford an opportunity to explain. If, after
hearing the response and making further investigation as warranted, it
determines that the person failed to disclose, it shall take appropriate
disciplinary and corrective action.

**A.8 Records.** The minutes shall record the names of persons who disclosed or
were found to have a financial interest, the nature of the interest, any action
taken to determine whether a conflict existed, the Board's decision as to
whether a conflict existed, the names of persons present for discussions and
votes, the alternatives considered, and a record of the vote.

**A.9 Annual statements.** Each covered person shall annually sign a statement
affirming that the person has received, read, and understands this policy,
agrees to comply with it, and understands that the Corporation is charitable and
must engage primarily in activities that accomplish its exempt purposes, and
disclosing any financial interests.

**A.10 Periodic reviews.** The Corporation shall periodically review whether
compensation arrangements are reasonable and the result of arm's-length
bargaining, and whether partnerships, joint ventures, and arrangements with
management organizations conform to written policies, are properly recorded,
reflect reasonable payments for goods and services, further charitable purposes,
and do not result in inurement or impermissible private benefit.

# APPENDIX B — WHISTLEBLOWER AND NON-RETALIATION POLICY

**B.1 Purpose.** Every director, officer, employee, volunteer, and contractor is
expected to observe high standards of business and personal ethics and to comply
with applicable law. This policy encourages and protects those who report
suspected wrongdoing.

**B.2 What to report.** Suspected fraud, theft, misuse of funds or assets,
falsification of records, violation of law or of the Corporation's policies,
danger to health or safety, harassment or abuse, or retaliation.

**B.3 How to report.** Reports may be made to the reporter's supervisor, the
Executive Director, or — where the report concerns the Executive Director, a
director, or the reporter is uncomfortable reporting internally — directly to the
Chair of the Board or the Treasurer. The Corporation shall publish current
contact routes for each of these in the Employee Handbook and to volunteers.
Anonymous reports are accepted and will be investigated to the extent the
information allows.

**B.4 No retaliation.** No one who in good faith reports a suspected violation
shall suffer harassment, retaliation, discipline, or adverse change in their
service or employment. Retaliation is itself a violation of this policy and is
grounds for discipline up to and including termination. This protection applies
to a report made in good faith even if the concern turns out to be unfounded; it
does not protect a knowingly false report.

**B.5 Confidentiality.** Reports will be kept confidential to the extent
possible consistent with the need to investigate and with legal requirements.

**B.6 Handling.** The Executive Director, or the Chair where the report concerns
the Executive Director, shall promptly investigate or cause an investigation,
document the findings, and report to the Board on the disposition of any
credible report of financial wrongdoing, illegality, or harm to a person.

**B.7 Records.** No document relevant to a report shall be destroyed while the
matter is open (Appendix C).

# APPENDIX D — GIFT ACCEPTANCE POLICY

**D.1 General.** The Corporation accepts gifts that further its purposes. It
may decline any gift, and shall decline a gift that is inconsistent with its
mission, that would expose it to liability, unmanageable cost, or reputational
harm, that carries conditions it cannot honor, or whose source is unlawful.

**D.2 Accepted without special review.** Cash, checks, card and electronic
payments, recurring pledges, and publicly traded securities.

**D.3 Requiring Executive Director approval.** Non-cash gifts of tangible
property the Corporation will use; gifts with a stated purpose narrower than
general support; gifts conditioned on naming, publicity, or a specific vendor;
in-kind services; and any gift that obligates the Corporation to future
expenditure.

**D.4 Requiring Board approval.** Real property; closely held or restricted
securities; cryptocurrency and other digital assets; gifts subject to a life
income, remainder, or annuity interest; gifts of property carrying debt,
environmental risk, or ongoing maintenance obligations; and any gift, or series
of related gifts from one source in a fiscal year, exceeding the threshold the
Board sets by resolution.

**D.5 Anonymous and restricted gifts.** Anonymous gifts are accepted where the
Corporation can satisfy its anti-money-laundering and record-keeping obligations.
A gift restricted to a chapter or purpose is handled under Bylaws Section 9.5.

**D.6 Acknowledgment and substantiation.** The Corporation shall promptly
acknowledge every gift in writing, and shall provide the contemporaneous written
acknowledgment required by Section 170(f)(8) of the Code for gifts of $250 or
more, stating whether any goods or services were provided in return and their
good-faith estimated value. Where a donor receives goods or services in exchange
for a payment over $75, the Corporation shall make the quid pro quo disclosure
required by Section 6115. The Corporation does not appraise gifts or state the
value of non-cash property; it may sign Form 8283 as donee and shall file Form
8282 where required.

**D.7 Donor privacy.** Donor records are confidential and are used only for the
Corporation's own purposes. The Corporation does not sell, rent, or trade donor
lists. A donor may request anonymity in public materials, and the Corporation
shall honor it.

**D.8 Refusal and return.** The Executive Director may return a gift where
acceptance was mistaken or where retention would harm the Corporation, and shall
report any return over the Board's threshold to the Board.

## What changed from the 2021 bylaws

The 2021 document was a stock template with several problems worth naming, since
this draft is judged against it.

| # | 2021 | This draft | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Article 1 governs "**We Love Neighbor, Inc.**" — a different organization's name left in the template | Global Echo Charitable Organization throughout, with Public Worship as a registered trade name (§1.2) | The operative name clause did not name the corporation |
| 2 | Cites the "State of Maryland Nonprofit Corporation Act" | Cites the Maryland General Corporation Law as applied to nonstock corporations (§1.5) | Maryland has no separately titled Nonprofit Corporation Act; counsel should confirm the citation |
| 3 | No purpose clause, no inurement clause, no lobbying/political limits, **no dissolution clause** | Articles II and XVI | These are the provisions the IRS looks for; a missing dissolution clause is the classic exemption defect |
| 4 | No conflict-of-interest policy, whistleblower policy, document retention policy, or gift acceptance policy | Appendices A–D, incorporated by Article XII and XIII | Form 990 Part VI asks about each; the first three are effectively expected of a public charity |
| 5 | No compensation-approval procedure | §12.6, following Treas. Reg. § 53.4958-6 | Establishes the rebuttable presumption of reasonableness and protects against § 4958 penalties |
| 6 | President is CEO **and** "officers shall not receive any salary" | Board officers (Chair/Secretary/Treasurer) are uncompensated governance roles; the **Executive Director** is the chief executive and may be paid (Articles VI, VIII) | The old text made it impossible to pay the person actually running the organization |
| 7 | Board minimum of five, three-year terms, no term limits, no independence rule, removal by majority of remaining directors | Minimum three, maximum fifteen, staggered terms, three-term limit, majority-independent board, removal by two-thirds with notice and a hearing (Article IV) | The five-director floor did not match reality; independence and term limits are what make a small founder-led board credible |

| 9 | Notice by "telegraph"; two days' notice; no remote-participation rule beyond conference telephone | Electronic notice, five days (24 hours for urgent), video participation, electronic signatures (Article V) | Modernization |
| 10 | Nothing about chapters | Article IX | The organization's actual structure — chapters are programs of the corporation, not entities, and that needs to be written down before someone opens a bank account in a chapter's name |

## Open decisions

These are the calls the founder and Board must make before adoption; the draft
carries a reasonable default for each, marked ▶.

1. **Board size and composition.** ▶ 3–15, majority independent. Who are the
   initial directors, and are there enough independent ones to seat a real
   board? This is the largest gap between the draft and reality today.
2. **Is the Executive Director a director?** ▶ Not required either way. If yes,
   the ED counts against the independence majority and never votes on their own
   compensation or evaluation.
3. **Term limits.** ▶ Three consecutive three-year terms, then a year off. A
   founder-led organization may want a founder carve-out; if so, it should be
   explicit rather than implied.
4. **Contract approval threshold** (§10.1) and **dual-authorization threshold**
   (§10.3). ▶ Set by Board resolution rather than baked into the Bylaws, so they
   can move without an amendment. Suggested starting points: Board approval over
   $10,000 or any multi-year commitment; dual authorization over $2,500.
5. **Gift acceptance thresholds** (Appendix D.4). ▶ Board approval for any gift
   or related series over $25,000, and for all real property, digital assets, and
   split-interest gifts regardless of amount.
6. **Level of financial examination** (§10.9). ▶ Compilation or review now,
   audit when revenue or a funder requires it. Maryland's charitable-solicitation
   thresholds should drive this; counsel to confirm current figures.
7. **Meeting cadence.** ▶ Quarterly. Monthly is unrealistic for a volunteer
   board; twice a year is too thin for an organization moving this fast.
8. **Statement of faith.** Article I references the Corporation's religious
   character but does not incorporate a doctrinal statement. Decide whether one
   is adopted, and whether it is incorporated by reference into these Bylaws (it
   then becomes hard to change) or adopted as a Board policy (easier to revise).
9. **Chapter cost sharing** (§9.4). The Bylaws point to the Operating Manual for
   the basis; the Board should ratify the current model — the 15% City Launch
   Fund share — by resolution so the manual is standing on delegated authority.
10. **Assumed name registration.** Confirm that "Public Worship" is properly
    registered as a trade name in Maryland and in every state where chapters
    solicit, and that the domains, payment accounts, and social handles are held
    in the Corporation's name (§9.6).
